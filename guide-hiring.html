How to Hire a Filipino Worker: A Complete Employer Guide

Foreign employers looking to hire Filipino workers must navigate a government-regulated process overseen by the Philippine Department of Migrant Workers (DMW). This guide explains what the process involves — and why working with a licensed agency is the most efficient path.

DMW Compliance Filipino Talent Global Hiring

Executive summary

  • High technical alignment. Filipino workers hold standardised national vocational credentials (TESDA), communicate confidently in English, and adapt quickly to regulated, safety-conscious worksites.
  • Regulated on both sides. Deployment is only legal when your side's employment requirements and the Philippine DMW's accreditation and exit-clearance framework are satisfied together.
  • A licensed agency is the required path. Philippine law prohibits direct hiring for most categories — a DMW-licensed agency such as Link Asia (License No. DMW-067-LB-03312023-R) acts as your legal counterpart in Manila.

Why Filipino Workers?

Filipino workers are consistently among the most sought-after in the global labour market, and the reasons are structural rather than sentimental. English is a language of instruction in Philippine schools, so site inductions, safety briefings and written procedures land without translation friction. Vocational skills are certified under a single national system — the Technical Education and Skills Development Authority (TESDA) — which gives employers a readable standard: a welder with the right National Certificate has passed the same assessment whether trained in Luzon or Mindanao.

Just as importantly, overseas employment is a mature institution in the Philippines. The government regulates it, banks are built around it, and workers approach a foreign contract as a serious long-term commitment — which shows up for employers as low turnover and high contract-completion rates.

The Role of a DMW-Licensed Recruitment Agency

Foreign employers cannot simply recruit Filipino workers directly. Philippine law channels overseas hiring through agencies licensed by the Department of Migrant Workers, and for good reason: the licence makes someone in the Philippines legally accountable for every deployment — for the truth of the job offer, the standard of the contract, and the worker's documentation.

The agency acts as your authorised representative in the Philippines: sourcing and screening candidates against your specifications, running trade tests where the role calls for them, preparing the contract for government verification, and walking your company through employer accreditation if this is your first Filipino hire.

Compliance note for employers. A Filipino worker cannot legally leave the country for a foreign job without government exit clearance — the Overseas Employment Certificate (OEC), checked at the airport by immigration officers. Every step that leads to it, from employer accreditation to contract verification, has to be done correctly and in order. This is the single most common source of deployment delay for first-time employers, and the part a licensed agency exists to get right.
