Hiring Household Service Workers: Standards and Protections

Household service workers are among the most regulated categories in Philippine overseas employment — deliberately. Understanding the standards protects your household and makes for a placement that lasts.

Household StaffHSWCompliance

Executive summary

  • The category is specially protected under Philippine deployment rules, with stricter age, contract and welfare standards than most others.
  • Link Asia deploys household service workers aged 24 and above, screened and documented for household placements.
  • Clear expectations make successful placements. Duties, rest days, accommodation and communication set out upfront prevent nearly every common dispute.

Why this category has extra rules

A household worker lives inside the employer's home, which is why the Philippine government applies heightened standards to the category — on age, on contract terms and on welfare. Employers should read these rules as quality assurance: they filter the pipeline toward mature, prepared candidates and set the placement up to succeed.

At Link Asia, household service worker candidates are at least 24 years old, undergo document verification and interviews, and complete the government's required pre-departure preparation before deployment.

What a compliant placement looks like

The employment contract is verified on the Philippine side before deployment, and it covers the matters that later become disputes when left vague: specific duties, working hours and rest, accommodation, food, medical care and communication. Candidates are briefed on the destination's household norms; employers receive a profile that reflects an actual interview, not just a form.

Setting the household up to succeed

The strongest predictor of a lasting placement is expectation-matching before deployment. Be specific in your requirements — children or elderly care, cooking, languages, pets, house size — and honest about the household routine. We match against what you actually describe, and the worker arrives knowing the job she accepted.

A note on recruitment channels. Household workers are a common target of illegal recruiters. Hiring through a DMW-licensed agency is not just the legal path — it is the only path where someone in the Philippines is accountable to you and to the worker.
