Filipino Nurses and Caregivers: Reading the Credentials

The Philippines is one of the world's leading sources of healthcare workers. But a nurse and a caregiver are different professions with different credentials — and employers who know how to read them hire better.

HealthcareNursesCaregivers

Executive summary

  • Registered nurses hold a PRC licence — a bachelor's degree in nursing plus a national board examination, regulated by the Professional Regulation Commission.
  • Caregivers are TESDA-certified — typically holding a Caregiving National Certificate, a vocational credential focused on daily care.
  • Match the credential to the role. Hospitals and clinics need PRC-licensed nurses; home care and assisted living are often best served by certified caregivers.

Two professions, two credential paths

Philippine registered nurses complete a four-year Bachelor of Science in Nursing and pass the national licensure examination administered by the Professional Regulation Commission (PRC). The PRC licence is verifiable, renewable and the non-negotiable marker of a hospital-ready nurse. Many also carry specialty experience — dialysis, operating room, intensive care — documented through hospital employment records.

Caregivers follow a vocational path: training and assessment under TESDA, most commonly the Caregiving National Certificate. This credential certifies competence in daily living assistance, basic health monitoring and client safety — the core of home and elderly care.

What to look for on a CV

For nursing roles, look for the PRC licence number and verify it, then weigh hospital tenure and unit experience over certificate count. For caregiving roles, look for the TESDA certificate plus actual care experience — household or institutional — and language comfort appropriate to your clients.

Be cautious with profiles that blur the two: a caregiver is not a nurse, and deploying one into a role that requires clinical judgement is a risk to patients and to your organisation. A good agency will tell you when the role you have described needs the other credential.

Why the world hires Filipino healthcare workers

English-language clinical education, a strong caring culture and decades of overseas placement history mean Filipino nurses and caregivers integrate quickly into foreign health systems. Demand from hospitals and care facilities across our corridors has been consistent for years, and the pipeline of licensed graduates remains among the deepest anywhere.
