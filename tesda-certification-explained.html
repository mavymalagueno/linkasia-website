TESDA Certification: What It Actually Certifies

TESDA National Certificates appear on nearly every skilled Filipino worker's CV. Here is what the levels mean, how the assessment works, and what the certificate does and does not tell you as an employer.

TESDASkilled TradesCertification

Executive summary

  • NC I to NC IV mark rising competency levels, from routine tasks under supervision to complex work with supervisory responsibility.
  • Certificates follow hands-on assessment by accredited assessors against national competency standards — not classroom attendance alone.
  • The certificate is a floor, not a ceiling. It verifies baseline competence; actual project experience still separates candidates.

What TESDA is

The Technical Education and Skills Development Authority (TESDA) is the Philippine government authority for technical-vocational education. It defines competency standards for hundreds of qualifications — welding, carpentry, electrical installation, heavy equipment operation, caregiving, cookery and many more — accredits training centres, and certifies workers through assessment.

A National Certificate (NC) is issued only after a candidate demonstrates the required competencies to an accredited assessor. That is the detail employers should care about: the credential reflects observed performance, not just completed coursework.

Reading the levels

NC I and NC II cover work performed under supervision — NC II is the common benchmark for deployable trade workers, covering the full routine scope of a trade. NC III adds independent judgement and more complex tasks; NC IV adds planning and supervisory responsibility. For most overseas trade positions, NC II in the relevant qualification is the baseline worth requiring, with NC III and above marking candidates for lead-hand and supervisory roles.

What the certificate does not tell you

A certificate verifies competence at assessment time in a standard setting. It does not tell you how a welder performs on your alloys, at your production pace, to your inspection standard. That is why serious recruitment pairs credential checks with trade testing against the employer's own specifications — which we arrange at accredited testing facilities, with employers welcome to observe live or by video.

Employer tip. Name the exact TESDA qualification and level in your job order — for example 'SMAW NC II' rather than 'welder'. Precision in the job order is the single cheapest way to raise the quality of the shortlist.
